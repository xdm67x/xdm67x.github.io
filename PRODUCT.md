## Platform

web

## Stack

Rust + wgpu (WebGPU) compiled to WebAssembly, Trunk as the only build tool.
The front end is plain ES modules (`web/`) with no bundler or transpiler, and
`index.html` holds all content as real DOM. The background film is a seamless
loop generated by a dependency-free Rust program (`tools/loopgen.rs`) and
encoded with ffmpeg; the encoded result ships in `public/film/`. Static site
built to `dist/`, deployed to GitHub Pages on push to `main`. No Node, Bun, npm
or asset pipeline in the toolchain.

## Users

Recruiters, engineers, and potential collaborators evaluating Mehmet Ozkan for

Personal portfolio for Mehmet Ozkan: present his projects and experience, land

## Positioning

A portfolio that cuts like a game menu. Four full-frame slides sit over one
continuous looping film of a night sea; moving between them is a single hard
transition — the page is torn into bands, pushed through the lens, and the next
one lands oversized, snapping into place behind a flash and a shockwave. The
same choreography is playing in the live 3D scene behind the page, on the same
clock, so the document and the world come apart together.

Nothing about the page announces its own technology: no stack labels, no
renderer readout. The work is the subject.

## Operating Context

Deployed at https://xdm67x.github.io via GitHub Actions on push to `main`. Must
perform on mid-range laptops and on phones. The world is progressive
enhancement: without WebGPU, or without scripting, the four slides are a plain,
complete, readable portfolio.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Single-page deck, four slides: hero (identity), experience, projects,
  contact. Navigation by wheel, trackpad, touch flick, swipe, <kbd>↑</kbd>/
  <kbd>↓</kbd>/<kbd>space</kbd>/<kbd>1</kbd>–<kbd>4</kbd>, the glass nav, and
  the browser's back button (each slide is a history entry).
- The transition ("the cut") is one authored piece of motion, 0.92 s, driven by
  a single `progress` value in the front end that is handed to the Rust renderer
  every frame as `set_deck(from, to, progress)`. The world can never drift out
  of sync with the page.
- The background is one looping film of a night sea, generated for the page:
  seamless because every wave component advances by a whole number of cycles per
  loop, not because it is cross-faded. It is a `<video>`, muted, looping,
  `playsinline`, with a poster and no audio track.
- The 3D layer is a pool of 176 glass pieces — a core, three hairline rings and
  172 plates — arranged four different ways, one per slide: an orbit, three
  columns over a field of fallen plates, a lattice of tiles, concentric rings
  around a signal. A slide change flies every piece from one arrangement to the
  next through a burst that peaks where the impact lands.
- Four room colours, all cut from the same deep blue (abyss, deep sea, night
  blue, glacier), each with one accent (azure, cyan, iris, ice). The renderer
  owns the table; the page reads it, so world and document cannot disagree.
- Three small labels are pinned into the world from the renderer's per-frame
  anchor projection (a span for experience, an index for projects, a position
  for contact). They annotate; they never repeat what the document says.
- Real, factual content only — the project list (`web/data.js`) and the CV
  (repo `xdm67x/resume.pdf`, private). No invented titles, dates or claims.
- No résumé button and no renderer readout in the chrome (user decision,
  2026-10-15); the page prints as a plain document instead.
- No gaming vocabulary in the furniture: no score, no HUD, no pixel fonts. The
  game register lives in the motion, never in the words.

## Product Principles

- The cut is the product's signature: one transition, tuned, used everywhere.
- One clock. The page and the world are driven by the same number, so the two
  layers are one movement rather than two animations near each other.
- The document is the product: every fact lives in the DOM, and the world is a
  presentation of it. Nothing readable depends on the renderer, the film or
  scripting.
- Atmosphere is the film's job; the world adds structure, never noise. When the
  background and the geometry compete, the background wins.
- Performance and accessibility are part of the craft: reduced-motion users get
  a still film and a plain slide change, and the site works without WebGPU.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`: no cut, no tear, no film motion, no drift —
the slide simply changes. Keep text contrast at 4.5:1 or better over the film,
which is graded for it. Keyboard: every slide is reachable with the arrow keys,
<kbd>space</kbd> and <kbd>1</kbd>–<kbd>4</kbd>, focus moves to the landed
slide's heading, off-screen slides are `inert`, `:focus-visible` rings
throughout, and every decorative layer (film, canvas, tear, pinned labels) is
`aria-hidden`. Slide changes are announced in a live region. Print produces a
plain, ordered document.
